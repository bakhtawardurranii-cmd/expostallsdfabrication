Exhibition Stall Checklist: From Brief To Show Day

Six stages, one checklist: everything to confirm before, during and after the build so your stall is ready when the doors open.

By Expo Stalls Fabrication · Published 6 October 2026

Start planning your exhibition stall at least 2 to 3 weeks before the show, and earlier for large or complex builds and peak exhibition months. The sequence is always the same: confirm the space and rules, write the brief, approve the design, fabricate, install, exhibit, dismantle. This checklist lays that sequence out in order so nothing is left to the last week.

1. Before You Book A Fabricator

  • Confirm your stand number, size and number of open sides with the organiser
  • Download the exhibitor manual and note height limits, flooring, electrical and fire rules
  • Decide the format: shell scheme, modular or custom
  • Set a budget range and check typical starting prices in the cost guide
  • List your goals for the show: leads, product demos, meetings or brand launch

Not sure about the format? Read shell scheme vs custom exhibition stall. For budgets, use the exhibition stall cost guide.

2. Write The Brief And Shortlist

  • Booth size, hall, show dates and move-in and move-out windows
  • Logo files, brand colours and any existing artwork
  • Products to display, how many people will staff the stand and what storage you need
  • Meeting space, screens, demo areas and power points
  • A shortlist of three fabricators, each given the same brief

Use the fabricator scorecard to compare quotes, and the choosing a fabricator checklist for warning signs.

3. Approve The Design

  • Review the floor plan and visitor flow before looking at colours
  • Approve the 3D render, which shows how the stall will look from the aisle
  • Confirm lighting, graphics and furniture are in the drawing
  • Sign off the final drawing in writing so the quote and build match it

See how we handle layouts on our exhibition stall design page and previews on 3D exhibition booth design.

4. Fabrication

  • Send final artwork and any late changes early; late artwork is a common cause of delay
  • Confirm materials and finishes against the approved drawing
  • Ask for photographs of the stall in the workshop
  • Make sure a dry-build is done before the stall is shipped

The stages are explained in how expo stall fabrication works, and our approach is on the exhibition stall fabrication page.

5. Transport And Installation

  • Confirm transport dates and venue access times
  • Check electrical orders and venue-charged services with the organiser
  • Have the installation team on site during move-in, with the drawing to hand
  • Walk the finished stall and note anything to fix before doors open

What this stage includes is covered on exhibition stall installation.

6. During And After The Show

  • Check lighting, screens and graphics each morning before the doors open
  • Keep a list of faults and tell the installation team early
  • Plan dismantling and moving out within the organiser's window
  • Review leads and stand performance, and decide whether to reuse the stall at the next show

The Checklist On A Calendar

If you work backwards from the show, a realistic plan for a standard stall looks like this. Large or complex stands, and shows in peak months, need to start earlier.

  • At least 3 weeks out: brief sent, three quotes compared, fabricator chosen, floor plan agreed.
  • About 2 weeks out: 3D render and final drawing approved, artwork supplied, fabrication begins.
  • The final week: workshop dry-build and inspection, graphics and lighting fitted, transport booked, electrical order confirmed.
  • Move-in day: installation, walk-through and snag list before the organiser's cut-off.
  • Show days and move-out: daily checks, then dismantling inside the organiser's window.

These are planning guides, not guarantees. Design approval time and the organiser's move-in rules shape the real schedule, which is why the stages of expo stall fabrication are worth understanding before you commit to a date.

What To Bring To The Stand

A good stall still needs the small things. Pack these separately from the stall itself, so they are with your team on the first morning:

  • Product samples, demo units and spares, with a list of what is where
  • Printed brochures, price lists and business cards, plus a digital version on a tablet or QR code
  • Chargers, extension leads, adaptors and spare bulbs
  • Cable ties, tape, scissors and a basic repair kit for touch-ups
  • Exhibitor passes, the organiser's manual and your fabricator's contact number
  • A cleaning kit so the stand looks fresh each morning

Know The Venue

Every hall has its own loading rules, ceiling heights and power arrangements, so confirm them early. Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should I start planning my exhibition stall?

Around 2 to 3 weeks ahead is a reasonable minimum for a stall, and longer for large or complex builds or peak exhibition months.

What should I send a fabricator?

Booth size and open sides, hall and show dates, logo and artwork files, products to display, storage and meeting needs, and the organiser's exhibitor manual.

What causes most delays in building a stall?

Late design approvals, late branding artwork and unconfirmed hall details are the most common causes, more than the build itself.

Should I read the exhibitor manual?

Yes. It sets height limits, flooring, electrical and fire rules, and move-in and move-out windows that your design has to respect.

Who handles installation and dismantling?

With our service, the same team that fabricates your stall transports and installs it, supervises it during the exhibition and dismantles it afterwards.
